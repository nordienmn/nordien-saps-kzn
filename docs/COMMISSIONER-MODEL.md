# How the commissioner and Head Office fit, and what the board must do

Researched 2026-10-10 (web search; sources at the bottom). Statements marked [memory] are from general knowledge and were not looked up.

## The real structure (what the demo has to respect)

- **Command line.** The National Commissioner (Head Office, Pretoria) controls and manages SAPS. Each of the nine provinces is headed by a Provincial Commissioner, who is responsible for policing in the province, subject to the National Commissioner's power of control. Below that: districts, clusters, then stations (1,165 stations nationally as of March 2025; KwaZulu-Natal has about 184 stations in about 28 station clusters in the PMG material I found, which may be dated).
- **Six national divisions** feed the line: Visible Policing, Cluster Coordination, Detective Service, Crime Intelligence, Criminal Record and Forensic Science Services, Protection and Security Services.
- **Policing is a national competency, but provinces oversee it.** The Constitution (section 206) gives the province "monitoring, overseeing and liaising" functions. The Provincial Commissioner reports to the provincial legislature once a year on the state of policing. The province cannot command SAPS. [The KZN department that does this liaison is Transport and Community Safety, from memory; verify before naming it in the product.]
- **Independent oversight sits outside SAPS.** The Civilian Secretariat for Police Service monitors and evaluates SAPS performance and advises the Minister. IPID investigates alleged offences by SAPS and municipal police members. Both report to the Minister of Police. Parliament's police portfolio committee scrutinises SAPS.
- **GBV.** SAPS has about 185 FCS (Family Violence, Child Protection and Sexual Offences) units covering all 52 districts. They work closely with Thuthuzela Care Centres, which belong to the National Prosecuting Authority's sexual-offences unit, not to SAPS. So a GBV referral crosses an institutional boundary (SAPS to NPA/health), and that boundary must be visible.
- **Tips.** The national Crime Stop office and call centre sit at Head Office (Pretoria) and tips are followed up by SAPS members; Crime Stop is anonymous (08600 10111, MySAPS app, *134*10111#). So an anonymous tip's natural senior destination is Crime Intelligence at provincial or national level.
- **Municipalities** are a separate sphere of government. Municipal police services exist (also under IPID oversight [memory: Durban has a metro police service]). An unsafe-infrastructure report moves from SAPS to the municipality only by referral, never by command.

## What this means for the board (the owner view)

The commissioner owns the whole chain. A board of counters is not enough. It has to show **where the chain is failing and let the commissioner act on it**:

1. **"Needs your decision" first.** Escalated tips waiting for acknowledgement. Critical or high cases past their time limit. Directions past their due time. Urgent GBV referrals nobody has acknowledged. Calls ringing too long.
2. **Directions down the chain.** The commissioner can direct a case or tip to a unit (cluster commander, Flying Squad, Crime Intelligence, FCS, Detective Service) with an instruction and a due time. The unit acknowledges and completes it. Overdue directions come back to the commissioner's attention list. This is how ownership becomes visible.
3. **Accountability by cluster.** Received, open, closed, average time to first response and number past limit, per cluster. Cluster commanders answer to the commissioner for these.
4. **Access log.** Every opening of a GBV, SOS or tip record is logged and readable by the board. This is what an oversight body (IPID, Civilian Secretariat, Parliament) would ask for.
5. **Honest boundaries.** Referrals to Thuthuzela, shelters, health and municipalities are shown as referrals out, with their status, not as SAPS-controlled work.
6. **Head Office view.** The same board, labelled for the Provincial Commissioner or Head Office. Head Office would see every province; the demo has KZN only and says so.
7. **Daily brief.** A one-page summary the commissioner can send up to Head Office (what came in, what was handled, what is overdue, what was escalated, GBV referrals, access log count).
